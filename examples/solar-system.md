# The Solar System

The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a giant molecular cloud.

## Planets

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has no moons.

Venus is the hottest planet, with surface temperatures around 465 °C, because of its dense carbon dioxide atmosphere. It has no moons.

Earth is the only known planet with liquid water oceans on its surface. It has one moon.

Mars is known as the Red Planet because of iron oxide on its surface. It has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, which are probably captured asteroids.

Jupiter is the largest planet, a gas giant with a mass more than twice that of all other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. Jupiter has 95 confirmed moons.

Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system, made mostly of ice particles. It has 146 confirmed moons, the most of any planet.

Uranus rotates on its side, with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees, probably caused by a giant collision. It has 28 known moons.

Neptune is the windiest planet, with wind speeds reaching 2,100 km/h. It has 16 known moons, the largest being Triton, which orbits backwards.

## Other objects

Pluto was reclassified from planet to dwarf planet in 2006 by the International Astronomical Union.

The asteroid belt lies between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter.

Comets are icy bodies that develop tails when they approach the Sun.
